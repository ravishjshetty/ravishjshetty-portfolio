03 — Looking Closer

The problem wasn't just booking. It was everything around it.

01

Planning is scattered.

Event discovery, conversations and planning can happen across different places, making the overall experience feel disconnected.

02

Key details are hard to find together.

Users may need to compare event information across multiple sources before deciding whether to attend.

03

Plans don't always survive.

Coordinating with friends and keeping track of event details can make turning interest into an actual plan difficult.

These are working problem hypotheses, to be validated through user research.

How I Responded

I approached Eventra as a connected event experience rather than a standalone ticket-booking flow. The goal was to make discovery, decision-making, planning and booking feel like parts of the same journey.

05 — The Experience

The experience, step by step.

05.1 — Discover

Start with something worth discovering.

Challenge

A search-first experience can place the burden on users to already know what they want to attend.

Design decision

The discovery experience surfaces featured, popular and nearby events, giving users different ways to begin exploring.

Trade-off

A broader discovery feed helps users explore, but too many choices can be hard to scan. Home balances variety with clarity by grouping events into distinct sections and category shortcuts rather than one long list.

HomePopularNearbyCategories
05.2 — Explore

When you know what you're looking for, get there faster.

Search and categories sit in the open, not behind extra taps.

SearchCategoriesFiltersResults
05.3 — Decide

Before people book, they decide.

Challenge

Users need enough relevant event information to decide whether an event suits their interests and plans.

Design decision

Present the important event details in a clear hierarchy so users can evaluate an event before committing to a booking.

Trade-off

More information supports informed decisions, but excessive detail can overwhelm. The page leads with the essentials (date, location, price and organizer) so the decision doesn't depend on scrolling through everything else.

DateLocationPriceOrganizer
05.4 — Plan

Found something? Save it and plan around it.

Saved plans give people a place to collect outings and revisit them later.

SavedCalendarNotificationsShare
05.5 — Book

Once you're ready, booking gets out of the way.

Challenge

The booking process should help users complete their decision without introducing unnecessary friction.

Design decision

Keep the booking steps focused on the actions and information needed to proceed, with a clear confirmation at the end.

Trade-off

Fewer steps make a flow feel faster, but removing needed information or confirmation creates uncertainty. The flow keeps ticket, quantity and checkout in one path and ends with an explicit confirmation screen.

TicketQuantityCheckoutConfirmation
05.6 — Manage

The experience doesn't end after payment.

Your ticket stays accessible and your event stays organised.

06 — The Two Sides

Events have two sides.

Someone has to discover the event. Someone has to create it.

EVENTRA
Attendee

Help users find events, understand the details, book tickets and manage their attendance.

Discover↓ Explore↓ Decide↓ Attend
Organizer

Help organizers create events, provide event information, manage ticket sales and handle event-related tasks.

Create↓ Add details↓ Sell tickets↓ Manage

The two experiences serve different goals but belong to the same event ecosystem. The attendee experience focuses on discovering and attending events, while the organizer experience focuses on creating and managing them.

11 — Looking Back

I started with an event app. I ended up thinking about everything around the event.

01

What I learned

Designing Eventra encouraged me to think beyond individual screens and consider how discovery, decision-making, booking and event management connect into a complete experience.

02

Limitations

Eventra is a concept project and has not been released. Its assumptions and proposed solutions still need to be evaluated with relevant users.

03

What I'd do next

Validate the identified problems, test the main prototype flows, examine where users experience friction and refine the design based on the findings.

"Good product design isn't always about adding more. Sometimes it's about making the whole journey feel connected."

Eventra taught me to design the moments between the screens, not just the screens themselves.
