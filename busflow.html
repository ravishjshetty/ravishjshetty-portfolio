01 — The Origin

I wasn't trying to build another bus app.

I was trying to get my waiting time back.

I often found myself waiting at bus stands without knowing when the next bus would arrive. Even at familiar stops, I couldn't always tell which bus was coming or how long I would have to wait.

That uncertainty meant leaving early, waiting longer than necessary, or simply hoping the right bus would arrive.

I started thinking: what if I knew when the bus was coming before I left?

I could use that time differently — stay longer with friends, finish something important, or simply plan my day better.

Designed with Mangalore & Udupi in mind

BusFlow is a concept designed around the everyday local-bus experience in Mangalore and Udupi, where local and government buses are an important part of daily travel.

11 — What I Learned

Transit UX is about designing for uncertainty.

BusFlow taught me that a transportation experience isn't only about showing available routes. It's about helping users make confident decisions before and during the journey.

01

Permission shouldn't become a barrier

GPS makes the experience faster, but manual location selection keeps the journey accessible when permission isn't granted.

02

Empty states need direction

"No buses available" shouldn't be the end of the experience. Nearby stops and notification options give users a clear next step.

03

Timing is part of the experience

Departure time, ETA and time-to-leave aren't secondary details. They are what users make decisions around.

04

Design for the moment before the journey

I initially thought about BusFlow as a bus-finding experience. While designing it, I realized the more important problem happens before the bus arrives — deciding when to leave and how to use the waiting time.

I started with a bus problem.
I ended up designing around time.
